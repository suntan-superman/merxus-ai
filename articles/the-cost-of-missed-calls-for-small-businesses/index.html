The Cost of Missed Calls for Small Businesses

An unanswered call can be a missed booking, quote request, order, or question that never reaches your team. The cost differs for every business. Use your own call and sales records to estimate it without relying on industry-wide loss claims.

Estimate with your own numbers

A missed call is an opportunity, not a guaranteed lost sale.

Some callers leave a message, call again, or use another channel. Others may move on. Count only calls that represent a genuine buying opportunity, then estimate how many would have become customers using your own records. Multiplying every missed call by your average sale treats uncertain intent and conversion as certain revenue.

A transparent estimate

Use a simple formula.

missed calls × share that are qualified inquiries × share that convert × average sale value

This estimates possible revenue associated with those calls under your assumptions. To estimate contribution after variable costs, multiply by your gross margin. Subtract the monthly cost of a response option only when comparing that option. This is a planning aid, not a forecast.

Illustrative calculation

Replace every input with your own data.

For illustration only, suppose a business records 20 unanswered calls in a month. Assume 25% are genuine inquiries, 20% of those would book, and the average booking is $150: 20 × 0.25 × 0.20 × $150 = $150 in possible monthly revenue associated with those calls.

At an assumed 50% gross margin, the corresponding contribution is $75 before the cost of a service. These inputs are placeholders, not benchmarks or a claim about typical results. Use your call logs, booking records, transaction values, and actual margins—or track them for several weeks if you do not yet know them.

Compare practical alternatives

Choose a response path that fits the work.

Options include adjusting staff coverage, using voicemail with a clear callback promise, offering web booking or a quote form, setting up call forwarding, or using an answering service. Compare each option’s monthly cost and the work it removes or adds. Include setup time and cases that still require a person.

For a side-by-side framework, read AI receptionist vs. answering service vs. hiring.

Check whether callers can reach a human for urgent or sensitive matters and whether staff receive enough context to follow up. A lower missed-call count is not useful if requests are captured inaccurately or remain unassigned.

Review before you change

Track what happens after a call.

Review incoming calls, unanswered calls, completed callbacks, qualified inquiries, bookings, and response time. Compare similar days and hours, and note holidays or promotions. A caller who was answered may not book; someone who reached voicemail may still convert later. Avoid treating association as proof that the call caused a sale.
